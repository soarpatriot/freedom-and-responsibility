# Rich Habits Framework — Reference

Source: Thomas C. Corley, *Rich Habits: The Daily Success Habits of Wealthy Individuals* (富有的习惯). Based on a 5-year study of 233 millionaires (177 self-made) and 128 people living in poverty. Study thresholds: "rich" = annual income > $160,000 and net liquid assets ≥ $3.2M; "poor" = annual income ≤ $35,000 and net liquid assets < $5,000.

Use this file to ground classifications in the book's actual categories, reasoning, and survey statistics. All statistics below are from the book — do not invent others.

## 1. The 10 Core Rich Habits (keystone commitments)

These are the book's 10 keystone habits — the spine of the whole program. Keystone habits (核心习惯) are the most powerful because each one triggers a chain reaction in other, ordinary habits.

1. I will practice good daily habits every day, without fail. (Foundation of all the rest — the method: list your bad habits in one column, the matching good habits in a second column; the second column is your checklist.)
2. I will set goals and build habits around them (daily, monthly, yearly, and long-term goals), then pursue them wholeheartedly.
3. I will spend at least 30 minutes a day reading / self-educating.
4. I will do at least 30 minutes of aerobic exercise a day.
5. I will spend time every day strengthening relationships with other success-minded people.
6. I will live every day in moderation.
7. I will complete at least 70% of my to-do list each day ("do it now" — no procrastination).
8. I will live every day with a positive, enthusiastic, optimistic ("rich") mindset.
9. I will save at least 10% of every paycheck ("pay yourself first," before paying any bills).
10. I will control my thoughts and emotions every day.

Notable framing from Part 1:
- **Four kinds of luck**: (1) random good luck — uncontrollable (lottery win, inheritance); (2) random bad luck — uncontrollable (illness, lightning); (3) **opportunity** — the byproduct of good daily habits, like an apple tree you planted and tended; (4) **misfortune** — opportunity's "evil twin," the fruit of bad habits (job loss, failed investment, divorce). You can't control the first two, but habits fully determine the last two. Rich habits act "like a magnet for opportunity."
- **"Think, evaluate, then react"** is the wealthy person's response to difficulty; the unsuccessful do "ready, fire, aim."
- Example — **Jim Carrey** wrote himself a $20M check as a vision board when he was a nobody; his first lead-role payday was exactly $20M.
- Example — **Warren Buffett** has lived in the same ordinary house for 50+ years, drives himself to work, flies commercial: moderation and frugality in practice.

## 2. The 26 ranked Rich Habits (ordered by impact on life)

1. **Read/self-educate daily.** 88% of the wealthy read ≥30 min/day for self-education (not entertainment — only 11% read for fun); 63% listen to educational audiobooks/podcasts while commuting. They read biographies (58%), self-help (55%), history (51%).
2. **At least 30 minutes of aerobic exercise daily.** 76% exercise ≥30 min/day. Aerobic exercise grows brain cells (BDNF), delivers oxygen and glucose to the brain — it makes you healthier *and* smarter.
3. **Build relationships with other success-minded people.** 86% invest in lasting relationships with positive, success-minded people; 72% volunteer ≥5 hrs/month. Spend time with the optimistic and goal-oriented; minimize time with negative people.
4. **Pursue your own goals** (not goals others set for you). 80% of the wealthy prize pursuing goals; 55% spend ≥1 year pursuing a single goal. "Don't lean your ladder against someone else's wall." Pursuing your *own* dream unlocks passion.
5. **Dream first, goals second.** Dreams take time; realizing a dream starts with *finding* the dream, not just setting goals. (Impatient goal-only approaches lead to disillusionment.)
6. **Avoid wasting time.** Time is the one investment you can never recover — riskier to waste than money. 67% of the wealthy spend <1 hr/day on recreational internet, freeing time for goals, reading, exercise, networking.
7. **Sleep 7-8 hours a night.** 89% sleep ≥7 hrs. Sleep forms long-term memory (REM) and is where the subconscious feeds the conscious mind ("intuition").
8. **Rise early.** 44% of self-made rich rise ~3 hours before work starts. The "5 a.m. club" — knocking out your top 3 priorities early restores a sense of control (antidote to the helplessness that drives depression). A keystone habit.
9. **Develop multiple income streams.** 65% had ≥3 income streams before becoming wealthy. "3" is the magic number. Don't put all eggs in one basket; in downturns, multiple streams protect you.
10. **Never procrastinate.** Procrastination is a Poor Habit; even geniuses are ruined by it. Root cause is usually lack of passion for the work (only 13% of workers are engaged, per Gallup). 5 anti-procrastination tools: to-do lists, 5 daily must-do tasks, self-imposed public deadlines, accountability partners, and the "do it now" mantra.
11. **Find mentors.** 93% of the wealthy credit mentors; 68% call mentorship key to their success. Finding a mentor is a shortcut to wealth. Example — **Jamie Dimon** apprenticed under Sandy Weill (American Express → building a banking empire → Citigroup); Dimon became CEO of JPMorgan Chase. 5 kinds of mentor: parents, teachers, career mentors (aim 2 levels above you), books, and painful lessons from failure.
12. **Stay optimistic.** Every self-made rich person in the study was optimistic; 54% credit optimism as a driver of success. The brain's amygdala generates fearful "demon voices"; the wealthy play the **"What if?" game** to flip negatives to positives. Example — **Donald Trump** used "What if?" to silence doubts about *The Apprentice*.
13. **Paint a blueprint of your life.** 61% of self-made rich did "dream-setting" — writing a 500-1000 word "letter from the future" that primes the reticular activating system to spot relevant opportunities.
14. **Pursue your passion.** Every self-made rich person was passionate about their work. Passion beats education, IQ, capital, and skill. 6 shared traits: passion, persistence, focus, patience, work ethic, hunger to learn. (The book gives a 5-column exercise to find your life's purpose.)
15. **Persistence.** Self-made rich never give up; 27% failed in business at least once but rebounded. Persistence makes you unstoppable and attracts luck.
16. **Don't follow the herd.** Humans have a strong conformity instinct (evolutionary survival). The successful break away from the crowd and *create their own crowd* (e.g., Facebook, Google). Bigger crowd = more success.
17. **Display good manners / etiquette.** Thank-you cards, communication etiquette (don't blurt every thought, no gossip, no criticizing people to others), table manners, dress codes, proper introductions. 75% send thank-you/greeting cards.
18. **Mentor and help others.** Teaching deepens your own mastery and pulls people into your "crowd" as loyal followers — a long-term investment.
19. **Seek supporters, avoid destructive critics.** You need feedback, but destructive criticism (non-specific, non-constructive) only demoralizes. Surround yourself with people who give positive, specific, constructive feedback — people who've actually succeeded at what you're attempting.
20. **Believe in yourself.** Belief determines wealth: believe you're smart and you'll act smart. Beliefs (mostly formed in childhood) run like mini programs guiding behavior. Replace limiting beliefs with positive ones. Tools: letter from the future, writing your own obituary, dream/wish lists, goals around them.
21. **Help success-minded people succeed.** Helping others reach their goals builds your support team. Be selective — focus on the ~30% who are optimistic, goal-oriented, driven.
22. **Make thinking a daily habit.** Edison: "5% of people think, 10% think they think, the rest would rather die than think." The wealthy spend 15-30 min/day thinking alone (usually in the morning) across 10 topics: career, finances, family, friends, business relationships, health, dream/goal setting, problems, charity, happiness.
23. **Dare to ask.** Fear of rejection / fear of responsibility stops most people from asking for help. Asking is a numbers game — some say no, some say yes. (Corley himself overcame 3 weeks of fear to ask a wealthy man for help; the first call yielded a $150,000 offer.)
24. **Avoid negative influences / sources.** Beyond negative people — limit negative media (TV, news, websites). Media favors bad news because it resonates with innate fear; too much news breeds stress, anxiety, depression.
25. **Take calculated risks.** Self-made people overcome fear of failure and take measured risks. All invested their own money; 27% actually failed and started over. Ordinary people won't risk, so they don't become rich.
26. **Seek feedback.** Fear of criticism stops people from seeking feedback, but feedback tells you what's working. The ultra-wealthy test new ventures on a small scale first before committing all resources.

## 3. The 10 ranked Poor Habits (ordered by how badly they drag life down)

1. **Speculation (get-rich-quick / something for nothing).** 52% of the poor bet on sports weekly; 77% buy lottery tickets weekly. By contrast 84% of the wealthy never bet on sports and 94% never buy lottery tickets. There's no such thing as a windfall path to wealth.
2. **Excessive drinking.** 54% of the poor drink >2 alcoholic drinks/day; 84% of the wealthy drink less. 60% of the poor get drunk at least monthly. Alcohol dulls neural receptors (memory, thinking) and adds empty calories → overweight.
3. **Excessive TV / social media / time-wasting.** 77% of the poor watch >1 hr TV/day; 67% of the wealthy watch <1 hr. Social media dependence is the same Poor Habit. Efficient use of time distinguishes the self-made rich; wasting time distinguishes the poor.
4. **Negative mindset / victim thinking.** 78% of the poor think very negatively; 54% of the wealthy think very positively. The reticular activating system + hippocampus make reality conform to your beliefs — "I'm just born poor" literally programs the brain to keep you poor. Phrases like "rich people are all bad," "life is a battle" guarantee failure, and children inherit the pattern.
5. **Not reading / not learning.** 92% of the poor don't read to learn; 79% read only for entertainment. Growth requires daily reading and self-education.
6. **Bad relationships.** Only 4% of the poor associate with success-minded people; 96% associate with negative, toxic people. You can only succeed among like-minded people.
7. **A single income source.** The poor put all eggs in one basket — usually one job. Lose the job and there's no safety net.
8. **Drifting / no life plan (得过且过).** 95% of the poor have no life plan and never envision their future. Without a blueprint and long-term goals, you're "an autumn leaf drifting aimlessly in the wind."
9. **Spending more than you earn / debt.** 95% of the poor have no savings and most are in debt; 88% carry >$5,000 in credit-card debt. No savings + living beyond means = permanent poverty.
10. **Unhealthy habits.** Among the poor: 77% never exercise; 97% consume >300 junk-food calories/day; 69% hit fast food ≥3×/week; 86% don't floss daily; 69% eat candy >2×/week; 53% sleep <7 hrs; 66% are ≥13 kg overweight. Unhealthy habits breed "bad luck": eating disorders, heart disease, cancer, hypertension.

## 4. Cross-cutting principles for classifying unlisted habits

When the user gives a habit the book doesn't name explicitly, reason by analogy using these principles (and say you're doing so):

- **Keystone vs. ordinary habits.** Keystone habits (核心习惯) trigger chains of other habits — they're the most powerful and hardest to change. Rising early, daily exercise, and pursuing a goal are keystone. A single ordinary habit matters less on its own.
- **The habit seesaw (习惯跷跷板).** One end holds Rich Habits, the other Poor Habits. To become wealthy, >50% of daily habits must be Rich Habits; the middle class is roughly 50/50; the poor are >50% Poor Habits. Tilting the seesaw often takes only 2-3 habit swaps.
- **Habits determine the 4 outcomes.** 40% of daily behavior is habitual. Habits are the hidden cause of wealth/poverty, health/sickness, good/bad relationships, happiness/depression. Ask: does this habit compound *toward* those goods or *away* from them?
- **Time is the most valuable, non-recoverable investment.** Any habit that burns time with no return (idle scrolling, bar-hopping, binge TV) is a Poor Habit.
- **Moderation is a Rich Habit; extremes are Poor Habits.** The wealthy avoid emotional swings, overindulgence, and ostentation. Even "working too hard" at the cost of health/relationships violates the moderation principle.
- **Beliefs and emotions are triggers.** A negative self-belief or uncontrolled emotion is a Poor Habit even though it's "just thinking," because it spawns negative downstream behavior. Positive affirmations and emotional control are Rich Habits.
- **"Pay yourself first."** Saving before spending is a Rich Habit; consumption-first / debt-financed lifestyle is a Poor Habit.
- **Associate up.** Time with success-minded, optimistic people is a Rich Habit; time with chronically negative people is a Poor Habit.
- **Habits can even switch genes on/off (epigenetics)** and pass to children — so the stakes of a habit extend beyond the individual. Bad habits raise cortisol and activate disease genes; good habits do the reverse.

Golden rule: **every habit has a consequence.** Classify by the consequence the book's logic predicts, lead the impact note with that consequence, and never fabricate a statistic.
