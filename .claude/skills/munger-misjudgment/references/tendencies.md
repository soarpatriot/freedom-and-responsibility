# The 25 Tendencies of Human Misjudgment

Munger's full list from "人类误判心理学", each with his own example, the mechanism, and the antidote. The master key: these **combine** — look for the **lollapalooza** (several firing together → extreme outcome) rather than a single cause. Run **two-track analysis** on everything: (1) the rational interests/odds, (2) the subconscious tendencies forming the conclusion.

---

## 1. Reward & Punishment Superresponse（奖励和惩罚超级反应 / incentive-caused bias）
The most under-estimated force even by people who think they get it. "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." FedEx's night-sort fixed by paying per shift not per hour; Xerox salesmen pushing the worse machine because it paid more. **Agency cost** is the economists' name. Spawns rationalization of bad behavior for reward. *Antidotes:* rational accounting, anti-gaming system design, severe public punishment of exposed cheating, and — Walton's rule under reciprocation — remove the incentive entirely. Caution (B.F. Skinner): don't explain *everything* by incentives; that's the one-track "truffle hound" error.

## 2. Liking/Loving（喜欢/热爱）
We ignore the faults of, and comply with, what we love; distort facts to favor it; and favor people/things/actions merely associated with it.

## 3. Disliking/Hating（讨厌/憎恨）
The mirror: we ignore virtues of, and distort facts against, what we hate. "Politics is the art of marshaling hatreds." Fuels distortion in business rivalries and war.

## 4. Doubt-Avoidance（避免怀疑）
The brain rushes to a decision to remove doubt. Useful for prey under threat; dangerous for judges/juries — hence they're required to deliberate, not decide instantly. Triggered by **confusion + stress** (also a driver of religious conviction).

## 5. Inconsistency-Avoidance（避免不一致性 / commitment & consistency）
The brain resists change, preserving prior conclusions, loyalties, identities, roles ("first-conclusion bias"). Costly hardened beliefs held to the grave. Harnessed by manipulators: Franklin got a rival to like him by asking to borrow a book (the rival's mind reconciled the favor with liking him). Pretending to hold an identity/conclusion makes it become real (actors, hypocrites who improve, advocates who come to believe). *Antidotes:* hear the other side fully before deciding; require consideration of counter-arguments; "see one, do one, teach one" only what you've verified; don't chain a young mind to dubious ideology it will then self-reinforce.

## 6. Curiosity（好奇心）
Mammalian, strongest in humans; good education amplifies it, and curiosity helps counteract the bad effects of the other tendencies and brings lifelong learning and fun.

## 7. Kantian Fairness（康德式公平）
The categorical-imperative golden rule: we extend and expect fair, reciprocal behavior (one-lane-bridge courtesy, queuing, splitting a windfall). When expected fairness is denied, resentment follows. Munger credits it with helping end slavery.

## 8. Envy/Jealousy（艳羡/妒忌）
Evolutionary root in food-scarcity grab reflex; strongest among siblings. "It's not greed that drives the world, but envy" (Buffett). Law firms pay senior partners equally to suppress the chaos. Conspicuously *absent* from the psych textbooks Munger read — a taboo, because calling a stance envy-driven insults it as childish.

## 9. Reciprocation（回馈 / reciprocity）
Tit-for-tat, both hostile and favorable, mostly subconscious. The car salesman's free coffee costs you $500. Cialdini's experiment: a ridiculous request ("supervise delinquents for two years") refused, then a small retreat ("one zoo trip?") tripled compliance from 1/6 to 50% — the experimenter's small concession triggered the subject's reciprocal concession. Watergate: the AG assented to the burglary after a wilder proposal was withdrawn. *Antidote:* Walton's no-gifts procurement rule (not even a hot dog). Also the root of **guilt** (clash of reciprocation + reward-superresponse).

## 10. Simple Association（受简单联想影响）
Reflexes triggered by mere association, not actual reward. High price → assumed quality (works for power tools, oil-well pumps, luxury goods — status). Pretty girl on the shoe-polish tin; Coke ads never show a death; martial music recruits soldiers. Three dangerous sub-forms:
- **Past-success misassociation:** Napoleon and Hitler won elsewhere, then invaded Russia; the lucky gambler/investor repeats and busts. *Antidote:* scrutinize each past success for its luck; list new dangers absent from the old win.
- **Persian Messenger Syndrome / kill-the-bearer:** ancient Persians killed messengers bearing true bad news; modern CBS under Paley became famous for it, so Paley lived in a cocoon of lies. *Antidote:* consciously welcome bad news — Berkshire: "tell us the bad news promptly; only good news can wait" — and be informed enough that people fear you'll hear it elsewhere.
- **Stereotyping by group averages:** assuming the 90-year-old is dim or the old woman can't do math — trend doesn't predict the endpoint for an individual; the man who drowned in a river "18 inches deep on average."

## 11. Pain-Avoiding Psychological Denial（简单的、避免痛苦的心理否认 / denial）
Reality too painful, so facts get distorted until bearable. The mother who refused to believe her son's plane was lost. Worst in love, death, and chemical dependency (the addict's unreal optimism about his standing).

## 12. Excessive Self-Regard（自视过高 / overconfidence + endowment effect）
90% of Swedish drivers think they're above average. We overvalue our spouses, kids, and — once owned — our possessions (**endowment effect**) and our conclusions (the pork-belly futures holder loves his position more after buying). Lottery players bet more on self-picked numbers. *Antidotes:* favor track record over interview impressions (Munger's hiring committee; the HP/Fiorina warning); force objectivity about yourself and your things. **Tolstoy effect:** criminals think they didn't do it or were justified — people excuse fixable bad behavior instead of fixing it; face that this is a character flaw that gets you fired in demanding places. (Note: justified pride and self-respect are constructive — "never underestimate the man who overestimates himself.")

## 13. Overoptimism（过度乐观）
Demosthenes: "What a man wishes, that also will he believe." People are over-optimistic even when already doing well. *Antidote:* habitually apply Fermat/Pascal probability — the evolved gut estimate isn't enough.

## 14. Deprival-Superreaction（被剥夺超级反应 / loss aversion）
Losing $10 hurts far more than gaining $10 pleases; losing something *almost* obtained feels like losing the thing itself. The friendly Munger family dog that would bite only if you took food from its mouth. Drivers of its damage: territorial office politics; ideology defended by hating questioners (worse when the critic is an apostate); open-outcry auction overbidding (social proof + deprival — *antidote:* Buffett's rule, don't attend); near-miss slot machines ("BAR-BAR-lemon") designed to exploit it; throwing good money after bad to save a failing project (learn poker to fold); Munger's own $5.4M mistake declining the second block of Belridge Oil shares. *Antidote for ideology lollapalooza:* extreme courtesy + seating able skeptics (the Court; Bok's tenure vetoes).

## 15. Social Proof（社会认同 / social proof）
Automatically think/act as the surrounding crowd does — simplifies behavior and often works (follow the crowd to the stadium). The elevator-facing-backwards experiment. Teenagers follow peers over parents (so control the peer group, not the lecturing). Oil companies copy each other's bad acquisitions. Triggered most under **confusion + stress** (swampland-to-teachers sales, cults using rattlesnakes). Spreads bad *and* good behavior, and spreads via *inaction* too — the Kitty Genovese bystanders; passive outside directors; Serpico's corrupt precinct. *Antidote:* "learn how not to be like other people when they are wrong" — among the most valuable skills there is.

## 16. Contrast-Misreaction（对比错误反应）
The nervous system registers contrast, not absolute magnitude. The $1,000 dashboard that seems cheap next to a $65,000 car; the realtor showing three overpriced dumps first; the fake "was-price" discount (works even on those who know the trick). The frog boiled by slow heating; "a small leak will sink a great ship." *Antidote:* watch for the small step whose contrast with your current position is too small to notice.

## 17. Stress-Influence（压力影响）
Sudden stress spikes adrenaline and amplifies social proof. *Light* stress slightly improves performance; *heavy* stress causes dysfunction and depression (curable). Pavlov's flooded, nearly-drowned dogs: heavy stress flipped their temperaments and could break *any* dog; the most break-resistant were the hardest to restore; breakdowns reversed only by re-applied stress — relevant to sudden cult conversions and belief reversals.

## 18. Availability-Misweighing（错误衡量易得性 / availability）
The mind over-weights what's vivid and easily recalled. Hence the power of vivid images and of a graspable example. *Antidote:* deliberately underweight the merely-vivid; seek and check the less-available evidence; use checklists.

## 19. Use-It-or-Lose-It（不用就忘）
Skills atrophy without practice. Keep the multidisciplinary models in regular use, and practice the ones you use rarely — like a pianist.

## 20. Drug-Misinfluence（化学物质错误影响）
Substances degrade cognition and feed denial; avoid anything that risks dependency because the harm is so severe even a small probability isn't worth it.

## 21. Aging-Misinfluence（衰老—错误影响）
Cognition generally declines with age (some keep learning well into old age with continuous practice).

## 22. Authority-Misinfluence（权威—错误影响）
We over-follow leaders/authority (Milgram-style). The captain-and-copilot problem; the "follow the misreading boss" crash. Dangerous when the authority is wrong.

## 23. Twaddle（废话）
People emit meaningless talk that crowds out real work — the honeybee's garbled dance for nonstandard nectar. Keep twaddle-dispensers away from serious work.

## 24. Reason-Respecting（重视理由）
People comply far better when given a *reason* — even a flimsy one (the photocopier-queue experiment). Tied to Carl Braun's "always tell why." Also exploited by manipulators who supply meaningless reasons.

## 25. Lollapalooza Tendency（lollapalooza — the capstone）
Several tendencies acting **together** toward the same outcome, producing extreme, often non-linear results — the open-outcry auction, the Tupperware party, the cult, Milgram, Watergate, a great (or catastrophic) corporate culture. **This is the one to hunt for.** Big irrational outcomes almost never have a single cause; name the stack. Conversely, engineering good outcomes means pointing several tendencies the same constructive way (the best cultures, AA's ~multiple-tendency approach to sobriety).

---

Diagnostic reminder: name the *specific* tendencies, prescribe the *specific* antidotes, and — for any extreme outcome — identify the **confluence**. Knowing a manipulation exists is not full immunity; prefer a structural countermeasure (don't attend the auction; forbid the gift; welcome bad news) over mere awareness.
